Scout public land with Your World Hunt: a step-by-step plan

Listen uses your browser's built-in voice.

Public land can be some of the best hunting around, but it rewards the people who do their homework. Here is a simple scouting plan built around the tools in Your World Hunt, from the first look at a map at home to walking back to the truck in the dark.

1. Set your location, even from the couch

Allow location when the app asks, so the map, wind and moon follow you. Planning a spot from home, or no GPS fix? Tap the WIND @ line on the weather card (or SET LOCATION) and search a city, ZIP, address or latitude/longitude. Tap USE GPS to switch back when you're in the field.

2. Find the public ground with LAND

Open LAYERS and turn on LAND to paint public-land boundaries on the map. Use FILL, LINES and LABELS to tune how the boundaries look over satellite imagery, so you can still see timber, fields and water underneath.

With Premium, the live US public-land overlay loads while you're online. Hunting where there's no signal, or outside the US? Offline Land Packs are a separate add-on covering the US, Canada, Europe, Australia or worldwide, downloaded to your phone ahead of time.

One important note: land layers are an informational guide, not a legal survey and not proof of access. Always confirm boundaries and permission, and check your state agency for seasons, limits and rules.

3. Read the terrain on satellite and topo

Switch between satellite, streets and topo maps under LAYERS. Satellite shows cover and edges; topo shows ridges, saddles and drainages that funnel movement. Look for places where the two overlap: a timbered saddle, a creek crossing near a field edge, a bench just below a ridgeline.

4. Mark your stands and sign

Tap MARK in the bottom dock, pick a pin (stand, game, boat ramp and more), then tap the map to place it. Tap MARKS to see everything you've saved in a list. Long-press any mark for its pin options: directions, Weather Here, focus, or remove it.

The free version includes 1 stand, 2 boat ramps and 2 of each game mark. Premium unlocks unlimited stands, marks and saved track history.

5. Check the wind for that exact spot

The weather card shows wind speed and direction on its own line, plus barometric pressure with a rising, falling or steady arrow. Plan your entry so the wind carries your scent away from where deer will be.

Better yet, long-press a stand mark and tap WEATHER HERE to see conditions at that stand, not just where you're standing. Weather data comes from Open-Meteo, and Premium adds extended weather and alert options on top of the free wind, pressure and basic alerts.

6. Add moon and sun times to the plan

Tap the moon chip on the weather card to open the panel. Moon phase, moonrise and moonset, and sunrise and sunset are free. With Premium, the same panel shows deer feeding times: major and minor windows with a Good, Fair or Poor rating for the day. Treat them as one planning input alongside wind and pressure, not a promise.

7. Get there, and get back

Long-press a mark and tap DIRECTIONS. Choose how you're traveling: WALK for foot paths and trails, AUTO, ATV for tracks and dirt roads, or UTV for service and forest roads. Going off-trail? WOODS gives a straight bearing through the timber. Hunting by water? Mark your boat ramps, choose BOAT, and pick a put-in and take-out.

Once you're walking, tap TRACK to record a breadcrumb trail. When it's time to leave, GPS return shows the bearing and distance back to a mark, which is a big help in the dark.

8. Plan the whole trip

For a bigger hunt, Plan a Hunt builds an expedition with Hunt Days scored using moon, sun and a 10-day forecast (days 1–3 forecast, 4–7 trend, 8 and beyond outlook only), plus lodging search, kit, budget, a journal and reminders from dates you enter. The free version includes one expedition. Ask Hawk answers questions from your own trip data on your phone, and it never quotes seasons, limits or fees, so confirm those with your state agency.

Your data stays with you

Marks, tracks, trips and Ask Hawk chats stay on your phone unless you turn on optional sync. For more field reading, the Your World Hunt site has guides on public-land basics, playing the wind and more.
